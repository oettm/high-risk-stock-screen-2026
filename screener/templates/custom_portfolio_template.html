Executive summary

This is a custom portfolio analysis for a user-specified basket of 8 names (not the systematic screener's own selection): NVIDIA, Microsoft, Vertiv, TSMC, Broadcom, AMD, ASML and L'Oreal. Target weighting is equal-weight (12.5% each) since no conviction ranking was specified. Unlike the main report, this one respects a hard whole-shares-only constraint — no fractional shares — using a greedy allocation algorithm to get as close to equal-weight as EUR {{ "{:,.0f}".format(capital_eur) }} and integer share prices allow, with any un-investable leftover cash shown explicitly.

Badges: Fact (pulled live from Yahoo Finance, as-of date shown), Estimate (a calculation built on disclosed assumptions), and Opinion (qualitative analyst judgment, e.g. moat durability, business overview). Sector-peer valuation benchmarks and risk-rating percentiles are computed against the same 50-name curated universe used in the main screener, for methodological consistency.

Allocation — EUR {{ "{:,.0f}".format(capital_eur) }}, whole shares only

Equal-weight target (12.5% each) approximated via a greedy proportional algorithm that always buys the next share of whichever name is currently furthest below its target allocation — the standard heuristic for matching target weights under an integer-lot constraint. "Base" uses each stock's own forward EPS growth rate (capped at ±40% - see below - or a 6% fallback if unavailable) with no change in multiple. "Rate shock" de-rates the PEG-method bull multiple down to the sector peer median PEG. "FX headwind" applies the base case in local currency but converts at a EUR-appreciates-15% FX rate.

Growth-rate cap: forward EPS growth (forward EPS ÷ trailing EPS − 1) is capped at ±40% wherever it's used (composite score, PEG price targets, Base/FX-headwind scenarios). Trailing (ttm GAAP) EPS is often distorted by one-off charges - M&A amortization, stock-based comp, impairments - which can make the raw ratio explode into a number unrelated to the business's real growth.

Limitations & disclaimer

  • Data is pulled from Yahoo Finance via yfinance at run time; any gap is flagged as "N/A", never invented.
  • The valuation benchmark (sector peer median forward P/E) and risk-rating percentiles are computed against the same 50-name curated universe used in the main screener, not a third-party index.
  • Price targets are estimates built on two disclosed, peer-relative methodologies (forward P/E percentile; PEG-ratio percentile) — both relative-value, not intrinsic-value, methods. A prior version used a DCF instead of PEG; dropped for its terminal-value sensitivity producing misleading results on richly-valued, debt-carrying names - see the main report's limitations for the full rationale.
  • The Sharpe ratio uses the US 13-week T-bill yield (^IRX) as its risk-free proxy - a live, free, no-key series, but not a precise match for a EUR-based investor. Treat it as directionally informative, not a precision instrument.
  • Moat/competitive-advantage assessments and the "Business overview" are qualitative analyst opinion grounded in publicly known business characteristics, not derived from the live data pull.
  • Entry zones and stop-losses are derived mechanically from recent price history — not predictions, and do not account for upcoming earnings/news catalysts.
  • The whole-share allocation algorithm is a greedy heuristic, not a guaranteed-optimal solution to matching target weights - with only 8 names and a few hundred to low-thousands of euros per name, small rounding gaps versus the 12.5% target are normal and shown explicitly (see "deviation" column).
  • The backtest is a historical look-back on hand-picked names, not a forecast; the 5 forward scenarios are mechanical, formula-driven projections, not probability-weighted forecasts - no probability is assigned to any of the five, and real outcomes will differ from all of them.
  • This is educational research, not personalised financial advice. It does not account for your full financial situation, tax treatment, or any positions you already hold. Final investment decisions — including whether to invest at all — are yours alone.
